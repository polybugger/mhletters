# Scene 03 — Rome Before Romulus

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | L29–L42 |
| **Settings** | S3 Sinnett's study · S5 the bottle at the bottom of the sea · S6 Royal Society, 1662 · S7 Syracuse and "Rome before Romulus" · S8 the cave in the Himavats |
| **Characters** | Alfred Percy Sinnett, Koot' Hoomi (V.O.). Vision figures, all silent: Solomon, Francis Bacon, Robert Boyle, John Wilkins ("the Bishop of Chester"), Romulus, Archimedes, Edward Bulwer-Lytton |
| **Feeling** | Wonder |
| **Weight** | L |

## Purpose

- Widen the letter from Sinnett's test to his whole age. The question becomes whether the world is really as free-minded as it believes.
- Give the viewer a sense of deep time. Knowledge didn't begin with the Royal Society. Every founder had founders before him, back to races "now extinct".
- Introduce *Akás*, the force the letter says lies behind the phenomena and that science calls "a myth". This is the scene's moment of wonder, and it sets up scene 04's turn to science.

**Rules for this scene.** K.H. lines are word for word with line numbers, and "…" marks a cut. The vision figures are silent and act out only what the letter says about them. Sinnett doesn't speak: his reactions are physical. The history is played as the letter tells it.

## Beat outline

1. **Your best friends.** "Count them upon the fingers of your right hand." Sinnett actually counts, and stops short.
2. **The two vessels.** The age's boast: the genius of freethought, freed from "the narrow vase of dogmatism". Bigotry is the Jin, bottled and sealed "by the Solomons of science" at the bottom of the sea.
3. **"Aye; but is it verily so…?"** The seal on the bottle strains. One bubble rises.
4. **1662.** The Royal Society receives its charter, with Bacon, Boyle and the Bishop of Chester. Their "Invisible College" becomes visible.
5. **Ages before.** The camera pulls back through generations of men trying to "fathom" nature's secrets.
6. **Rome before Romulus.** Romulus ploughs the first furrow of Rome, and the plough turns up older stones.
7. **The Syracusian.** Archimedes draws in the sand. Behind him, fainter and older figures draw the same shapes, receding into the past.
8. **The *vril*.** Bulwer-Lytton's "Coming Race" on Sinnett's desk, then the *vril*'s glow passed hand to hand among a vanished race.
9. **The cave.** The cave of giants in the Himavats. The skeletons are labelled "freaks of nature", and the blue light of *Akás* rises around them.
10. **The question.** "How can Science hope to account for such phenomena?" Sinnett looks at the dug-up cup.

## Script

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — LATE MORNING

Thin sunlight now. Sinnett has moved to the armchair by the
window, the letter on his knee. He reads with more care than
before.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     The success of an attempt of such a kind as the one you
     propose, must be calculated and based upon a thorough
     knowledge of the people around you.                   [L29]
     It depends entirely upon the social and moral
     conditions of the people in their bearing on these
     deepest and most mysterious questions which can stir
     the human mind — the deific powers in man and the
     possibilities contained in nature.                    [L30]
```

```
FLASH — THE PICNIC (FROM SCENE 01)

The guests on the hillside, frozen mid-laugh like a
photograph. The skeptical guest's smile. The lady who cried
"It cannot be". The officer with the knife. Hume at his
mantelpiece, cup raised.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     How many, even of your best friends, of those who
     surround you, who are more than superficially
     interested in these abstruse problems?                [L31]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — CONTINUOUS

Sinnett raises his right hand. Folds one finger, a second. He
hesitates, a third. His hand stays half-closed.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     You could count them upon the fingers of your right
     hand.                                                 [L32]

He looks at his hand, and slowly lowers it.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Your race boasts of having liberated in their century,
     the genius so long imprisoned in the narrow vase of
     dogmatism and intolerance — the genius of knowledge,
     wisdom and freethought.                               [L33]
```

```
VISION — THE BROKEN VASE

A tall, narrow VASE of dark glaze, cracked. Golden LIGHT pours
from the crack and rises into a GENIUS: a tall figure of light,
arms opening, books and instruments turning in its glow.
Victorian applause, distant and proud.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     It says that in their turn ignorant prejudice and
     religious bigotry, bottled up like the wicked Jin of
     old,                                                  [L34]
```

```
VISION — THE SEAL OF SOLOMON

Torchlight on a stone floor. KING SOLOMON, robed and crowned,
presses his ring into the lead seal of a BRASS BOTTLE. Inside,
something dark and smoky beats against the metal.
```

```
VISION — BENEATH THE SEA

The bottle sinks through green-black water, turning slowly,
down to the sea floor. It settles in the silt.

Around it, standing on the sea bed as if it were a lecture
room, a ring of VICTORIAN MEN OF SCIENCE in frock coats. Each
wears a heavy SIGNET RING like Solomon's. They nod to one
another, satisfied: the Solomons of science.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …and sealed by the Solomons of science rests at the
     bottom of the sea and can never, escaping to the
     surface again, reign over the world as it did in days
     of old; that the public mind is quite free, in short,
     and ready to accept any demonstrated truth.           [L34]

The men turn away, one by one, their work done.

Close on the bottle. Silence. Then:

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (softly)
     Aye; but is it verily so, my respected friend?        [L35]

The lead seal creaks. A hairline crack. A single BUBBLE of
black smoke escapes and begins to rise, wobbling toward the
light far above.
```

```
VISION — A PANELLED HALL, LONDON — 1662

The bubble rises and bursts as candlelight: we are in a
wood-panelled hall. A royal CHARTER on a table, a great wax
SEAL hanging from its ribbon.

Around the table, in periwigs and lace:
FRANCIS BACON, grave and lordly.
ROBERT BOYLE, long-faced, attentive.
JOHN WILKINS, the BISHOP OF CHESTER, in clerical black.

A door behind them, half in shadow, is marked by nothing at
all: the Invisible College. As the charter is sealed, the door
fills with light and becomes an ordinary, visible doorway.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Experimental knowledge does not quite date from 1662,
     when Bacon, Robert Boyle and the Bishop of Chester
     transformed under the royal charter their "Invisible
     College" into a Society for the promotion of
     experimental science.                                 [L36]

The three men look up, proud, at a moment of founding.
```

```
VISION — AGES BEFORE

The camera pulls BACK from the hall, through its window, and
keeps pulling back, through time.

A dissolving chain of figures, each older than the last, each
bent over the same question:
— an alchemist at a furnace,
— a monk tracing stars in a manuscript,
— a robed astronomer on a tower,
— a herbalist in a forest,
— a figure by firelight drawing the moon on a cave wall.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Ages before the Royal Society found itself becoming a
     reality upon the plan of the "Prophetic Scheme" an
     innate longing for the hidden, a passionate love for
     and the study of nature had led men in every generation
     to try and fathom her secrets deeper than their
     neighbours did.                                       [L37]
```

```
VISION — THE FIRST FURROW

Dawn over hills. ROMULUS, young and armoured, drives a plough
behind two white oxen, cutting the sacred first furrow of Rome.

The plough blade strikes stone. He stops. Looks down.

In the furrow: the top of a carved STONE, worn and ancient.
Beyond it, half buried in the grass, the outline of older walls.
Someone was here first.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Roma ante Romulum fuit — is an axiom taught to us in
     your English schools.                                 [L38]
```

```
VISION — SYRACUSE

Sun on a courtyard. ARCHIMEDES, white-bearded, kneels over a
tray of sand, tracing circles and spheres with a stick, lost
in thought.

Behind him a fainter FIGURE kneels at the same kind of tray,
drawing the same shapes. Behind that one, another, fainter
still, and another: a line of thinkers receding into haze, each
older, each drawing the same circle.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Abstract enquiries into the most puzzling problems did
     not arise in the brain of Archimedes as a spontaneous
     and hitherto untouched subject, but rather as a
     reflection of prior enquiries in the same direction and
     by men separated from his days by as long a period —
     and far longer — than the one which separates you from
     the great Syracusian.                                 [L39]

Archimedes looks over his shoulder, as if sensing them. Sees
no one.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

On the bookshelf, a novel: THE COMING RACE. Sinnett's eyes
find it.

INSERT — a Victorian study. EDWARD BULWER-LYTTON, dandyish,
moustached, at his writing desk, pen raised, imagining.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     The vril of the "Coming Race" was the common property
     of races now extinct.                                 [L40]
```

```
VISION — THE VANISHED RACE

Darkness. A soft violet-blue GLOW passes from hand to hand
along a line of tall, shadowed FIGURES we never see clearly:
an ancient people, sharing the light as easily as bread.

One by one, the figures fade. The glow does not.
```

```
VISION — THE CAVE IN THE HIMAVATS

Snow at the mouth of a CAVE high in the mountains. The camera
moves inside.

Darkness, then shapes: SKELETONS, enormous, three and four
times the height of a man, lying in the rock as if asleep.
Ribcages like the hulls of boats. A skull the size of a cart.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     And, as the very existence of those gigantic ancestors
     of ours is now questioned — though in the Himavats, on
     the very territory belonging to you we have a cave full
     of the skeletons of these giants —                    [L41]

A British SURVEY PARTY's lantern swings into frame. A pith
helmet, a measuring tape, a notebook. A neat brass LABEL is
pinned beside one giant hand, and the party moves on, unmoved.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …and their huge frames when found are invariably
     regarded as isolated freaks of nature,                [L41]

The lantern goes. Darkness again.

Then, from the rock itself, a pale blue LIGHT rises, the same
glow as the vanished race's: soft, alive, filling the cave,
moving over the bones like breath.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …so the vril or Akás — as we call it – is looked upon
     as an impossibility, a myth.                          [L41]

The light swells, beautiful and silent, and fills the frame.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

The blue light fades into the ordinary daylight of the window.

Sinnett sits forward. His eyes go to the desk: the dug-up CUP,
earth still in the rim.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     And, without a thorough knowledge of Akás, its
     combinations and properties, how can Science hope to
     account for such phenomena?                           [L42]

He reaches out and touches the cup's rim. Holds it.

CONTINUE INTO SCENE 04 (opens on L43).
```

| **Sinnett is silent** | He reacts physically: the counting hand that stays half-closed (L32), sitting forward at the cave (L41), touching the cup (L42). This is the scene where he stops arguing and starts listening |
| **Two vessels** | The vase (freed genius, golden) and the bottle (sealed Jin, black smoke) mirror each other. Same scale and framing, opposite light. The single rising bubble is the hinge of the scene, on L35's "Aye; but is it verily so…?" |
| **Match cuts** | The bubble bursts into candlelight in the 1662 hall. The pull-back through the window becomes the pull-back through time. The *vril* glow among the vanished race is the same colour as the *Akás* light in the cave. The cave's blue light fades into the study's daylight |
| **Palette** | Study: warm daylight, brighter than scene 02. Visions: each era has its own tone. Gold for the vase, sea-green black for the ocean, candle amber for 1662, dawn rose for Romulus, white Mediterranean sun for Syracuse, and violet-blue for *vril* and *Akás*. That blue is the scene's colour of wonder and returns in later scenes |
| **The giants** | Keep the skeletons vast but calm, not horror. The survey party's brass label is the dry joke. The rising light is the scene's emotional peak |
| **The Invisible College** | A door with no sign, half in shadow, that turns into an ordinary lit doorway as the charter is sealed. Keep it simple and quick |
| **Text in frame** | Avoid readable text on the charter, the label and the book spine. Shapes only. The spoken lines carry the words |
| **Transitions** | The flash back to the picnic is a still frame from scene 01. Every vision begins from the letter's handwriting or a detail in the study, and returns with a hard cut. The final beat continues without a break into scene 04 |
| **Sound** | Study: clock tick, page turns. Vase: distant applause. Undersea: muffled pressure, the creak of the seal, the bubble's tiny pop. 1662: quills, wax, murmured approval. Romulus: oxen, wind, the plough striking stone. Syracuse: cicadas, a stick in sand. Cave: dripping water, then a low choral hum as the *Akás* light rises, the only music in the scene |
