# Scene 11 — Coda

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | None new. Two short reprises: L58 and L61 |
| **Settings** | S3 Sinnett's study (years later, empty) · a window in a London room (Blavatsky) |
| **Characters** | Helena Petrovna Blavatsky (silent), Koot' Hoomi (V.O., reprises only). Text cards |
| **Feeling** | Reflection |
| **Weight** | S |

## Purpose

- Tell the viewer, briefly and neutrally, what happened after the letter: Sinnett's book, the accusations against Blavatsky, and the unresolved question of who wrote the letters.
- Let the letter's own warnings echo against what followed, without saying whether they were prophecy or coincidence.
- End on the letter itself, lying on the desk, and on its central idea: "step by step, not at a rush."

**Rules for this scene.** Everything here is [context], taken from the analysis (§9, §11). The cards state events and the main positions, and give no verdict on authorship. K.H.'s two reprised lines are word for word from the letter. Nothing in the coda corrects the letter's history: it only records what came after. The scene is self-contained and can be cut without changing scene 10.

## Beat outline

1. **Years later.** Sinnett's study, empty, in still afternoon light. The letter on the desk.
2. **Card: 1881.** *The Occult World*.
3. **Card: 1883.** Sinnett leaves *The Pioneer*.
4. **Card: 1884–85.** The Coulomb affair and the Hodgson Report.
5. **Blavatsky alone.** At a window, as torn paper drifts past outside like snow. K.H.'s voice reprises "tearing you to shreds".
6. **Card: the dispute.** Theosophists rejected the report, and later critics challenged its methods.
7. **Card: still disputed.** Who wrote the letters is still argued over.
8. **Card: 1923.** The letters are published.
9. **The last image.** The letter on the desk, and K.H.'s voice once more: "step by step, not at a rush." Fade out.

## Script

```
FADE IN:

INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY (YEARS LATER)

The same room, changed. Dust sheets over the armchair. The
bookshelves half empty. Afternoon light lies still across the
floor. No one here.

On the desk, cleared of proofs: the LETTER, folded. Beside it,
the dug-up CUP, the earth long since dried in its rim.
```

```
TITLE CARD (over the empty study):

"In 1881 Sinnett published The Occult World, an account of
the Simla phenomena and of these letters."
```

```
TITLE CARD:

"In 1883 he lost the editorship of The Pioneer. His
Theosophical activities are said to have played a part."
```

```
TITLE CARD:

"In 1884 two former members of Blavatsky's household accused
her of faking phenomena. In 1885 a report for the Society for
Psychical Research, the Hodgson Report, concluded that she had
written the Mahatma letters, with help from others."
```

```
INT. A LONDON ROOM — DAY

A narrow room with a tall window. Grey city light.

BLAVATSKY, older, heavier, in black, sits alone at the window,
her chin resting on her hand, rings on her fingers, the long
chain at her neck: the pose of her portrait.

Outside the glass, scraps of torn PAPER drift slowly past,
falling like snow, as they fell through the spotlight in scene
05.

She watches them fall. She doesn't move.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (quiet, distant, a memory of the letter)
     …the higher and educated classes would go on
     disbelieving as ever, tearing you to shreds as before.  [L58]

The paper falls. She closes her eyes.
```

```
TITLE CARD (over Blavatsky at the window):

"Theosophists rejected the report. Later critics, among them
the handwriting examiner Vernon Harrison in 1986, argued that
its methods were flawed."
```

```
TITLE CARD:

"Who wrote the letters is still disputed."
```

```
TITLE CARD:

"Blavatsky died in 1891. In 1923, after Sinnett's death, the
letters were published as The Mahatma Letters to A. P.
Sinnett."
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY (YEARS LATER)

The empty study again. The light has moved across the floor.

Close on the folded LETTER on the desk. A breath of air from
somewhere lifts its edge, just slightly, and lets it fall.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (softly, the last words we hear)
     …the world's prejudices have to be conquered step by
     step, not at a rush.                                  [L61]

HOLD on the letter.

FADE OUT.

THE END.
```

## Visual and staging notes

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Character images** | Blavatsky `helena-blavatsky.jpg`. Her image's pose (chin on hand, rings, chain, black dress) is the shot itself, so this is the one place a portrait is used as-is. K.H. is voice only |
| **Card wording** | Plain, past tense and neutral, matching the analysis. Each card states an event or a position, never a verdict. The 1883 card says "are said to have played a part" because the analysis reports the cause only as "reportedly". The authorship card gives the report's conclusion and the main challenge to it, then states that the question is open |
| **Card sources** | 1881, 1883, 1884–85, the Harrison critique and "still disputed" come from the analysis (§9 timeline and §11). Blavatsky's death (1891) is from the analysis's people table. The 1923 publication date is from the analysis timeline. Check exact wording and dates before publishing |
| **The empty study** | Same room as the whole film, years later: dust sheets, half-empty shelves, the light moving across the floor between its two appearances to show time passing. The letter and the cup are the only things left on the desk |
| **Blavatsky's window** | A London room (where she later lived), grey light. The falling paper outside echoes the shreds of scene 05 without restaging the arena. She doesn't react. Stillness is the point |
| **The two reprises** | Only two K.H. lines, both already heard. L58 over Blavatsky ("tearing you to shreds") connects the warning to what happened. L61 over the letter ("step by step, not at a rush") is the film's last line, the letter's own answer to everything that followed |
| **The air lifting the letter** | One tiny movement in a still scene: the letter's edge lifting and falling, as if someone had just set it down or was about to read it. Not a supernatural sign, just air in an empty room |
| **Card design** | Simple, centred, the same typeface as the opening title card in scene 01, over darkened footage. Each card holds long enough to read twice |
| **Palette** | The empty study: muted, dusty gold. Blavatsky's room: grey London light, her black dress, white paper falling. Cards: pale text over the darkened image |
| **Pacing** | Slow and spare. Equal time on each card. A long hold on Blavatsky, and the longest hold of all on the letter before the fade |
| **Sound** | The empty study: a clock, very faint street sound. Blavatsky's room: London traffic muffled through glass, and the soft hiss of paper falling. Under the cards: a single sustained, unresolved tone. The last line over near-silence. A final note at the fade, the same note that closed scene 10, now quieter |
