# Scene 09 — Work With What You Have

## Purpose

- The letter becomes practical. After refusing the test, it gives Sinnett a plan: secure what you already have, publish it together, and stand behind it with your name.
- Turn Sinnett from disappointed to resolved. He picks up the pen in scene 08, and in this scene he starts arranging his evidence.
- Make the case for his own testimony. A witness "of well known character outweighs the evidence of ten strangers", and in India that witness is "the Editor of the *Pioneer*".
- Close with the letter's boldest parallel: a whole faith resting "for nearly 2,000 years" on one woman's testimony. It's staged with care, as K.H.'s claim (decision D6).

**Rules for this scene.** K.H. lines are word for word with line numbers, and "…" marks a cut. Patience Sinnett has one short [dramatized] line, off-screen. Stewart and Mary Magdalene are silent. "The note" is one of the phenomena the letter lists, and its details aren't known (analysis §12), so it appears only as a folded note, never explained.

## Beat outline

1. **A disappointment.** The letter admits it: it "will no doubt disappoint you". Sinnett almost laughs.
2. **Strategy.** "As a man well acquainted with the strategy". Sinnett's desk becomes a campaign map, with small flags on positions already won. Don't advance until they're held.
3. **What you already have.** On the real desk, Sinnett lays the evidence out together: the cup, a torn cigarette paper, a folded note. The brooch is mentioned, but not his to hold.
4. **Let them digest these.** Vision: breakfast tables across Simla, readers puzzling over a fuller *Pioneer* report.
5. **Justice to the woman.** Blavatsky alone at a window, tired, as the ridicule falls on her. Her name could be cleared.
6. **Isolated, injurious.** On the map, a single lone flag is knocked over. One report on its own does harm.
7. **Your lady's testimony.** Patience's voice from the doorway. Two witnesses, not one.
8. **Sir Donald Stewart.** Vision: a general's dispatch from the frontier, accepted on his word alone.
9. **The Editor of the *Pioneer*.** Sinnett realises the trusted witness the letter means is himself.
10. **One woman.** The empty tomb at dawn: a single woman, the only witness, and through the centuries, countless candles lit on her word.
11. **Resolve.** Sinnett draws a fresh sheet toward him, ready to write.

## Script

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett, pen still in hand from scene 08, reads on.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Your forthcoming letter I will not be able to fully
     answer without taking the advice of those who generally
     deal with the European mystics.                       [L81]
     Moreover the present letter must satisfy you on many
     points you have better defined in your last; but it will
     no doubt disappoint you as well.                      [L82]

Sinnett exhales: something close to a laugh.

                    SINNETT
          (dry, to the letter) [dramatized]
     It will.
```

```
VISION — THE CAMPAIGN MAP

The surface of Sinnett's desk transforms: the papers flatten
and spread into a great CAMPAIGN MAP of hills and passes, like
a general's table.

On it, small coloured FLAGS mark POSITIONS. Some are pinned
firm in the ground. Ahead of them, distant hilltops, unflagged,
beckon.

Opposite: a line of dark ENEMY markers, watching.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     In regard to the production of newly devised and still
     more startling phenomena demanded of her with our help,
     as a man well acquainted with the strategy, you must
     remain satisfied with the reflection that there is little
     use in acquiring new positions until those that you have
     already reached are secured, and your Enemies full aware
     of your right to their possession.                    [L83]

Sinnett's hand (in the vision) reaches for a far hilltop flag.
Stops. Pulls back. Presses down instead on a flag already
planted, firmly, making sure it holds.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Back to the real desk. Sinnett clears a space among the proofs.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     In other words, you had a greater variety of phenomena
     produced for yourself and friends than many a regular
     neophyte has seen in several years.                   [L84]

He gathers his evidence and lays it out, side by side, like
exhibits:

— the dug-up CUP, earth still in its rim;
— a torn CIGARETTE PAPER, kept between the pages of his notebook;
— a small FOLDED NOTE, creased, its writing turned away from us.

Beside them, his NOTEBOOK from the picnic, filled with his
handwriting.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     First, notify the public of the production of the note,
     the cup and the sundry experiments with the cigarette
     papers, and let them digest these.                    [L85]
     Get them to work for an explanation.                  [L86]
```

```
VISION — BREAKFAST TABLES, SIMLA — MORNING

A MONTAGE of houses across Simla. Verandahs, tea, toast. At
each table a reader with THE PIONEER, a fuller report now,
several columns:

— a colonel frowns, reads a passage twice, and calls his wife
  over;
— two clerks argue over it on a bench;
— a skeptical lady puts down her cup, and for the first time
  doesn't laugh.

Puzzled faces. Working for an explanation.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     And as except upon the direct and absurd accusation of
     deceit they will never be able to account for some of
     these,                                                [L87]

The SKEPTICAL GUEST from scene 01, at his own breakfast table,
reading about the brooch. He smiles his same knowing smile and
turns the page.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …while the skeptics are quite satisfied with their
     present hypothesis for the production of the brooch —  [L87]
```

```
INT. BLAVATSKY'S ROOM — DAY

A plain guest room in the Sinnetts' house. BLAVATSKY at the
window, alone, in black, a cigarette burning down unnoticed in
her fingers. On the table beside her: the mocking newspaper
from scene 01, folded open.

She looks tired. Older than she was at the picnic.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …you will then have done real good to the cause of
     truth and justice to the woman who is made to suffer
     for it.                                               [L87]
```

```
VISION — THE CAMPAIGN MAP

A single FLAG stands alone on a bare hilltop, far from the
others. The isolated report.

A gust. It topples and lies flat. The enemy markers edge
forward.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Isolated as it is, the case under notice in the Pioneer
     becomes less than worthless — it is positively injurious
     for all of you — for yourself as the Editor of that
     paper as much as for any one else, if you pardon me for
     offering you that which looks like advice.            [L88]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

A soft KNOCK. The door opens a little. We stay on Sinnett; we
see only a WOMAN'S HAND on the door frame and the edge of a
shawl.

                    PATIENCE SINNETT (O.S.)
          (gently) [dramatized]
     Alfred? You've been in here all morning.

                    SINNETT [dramatized]
     I saw it, didn't I? You saw it too.

                    PATIENCE SINNETT (O.S.) [dramatized]
     We both did.

Her hand rests on the frame a moment longer, then withdraws.
The door stays open.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     It is neither fair to yourself nor to her, that, because
     the number of eye-witnesses does not seem sufficient to
     warrant the public attention, your and your lady's
     testimony should go for nothing.                      [L89]
     Several cases combining to fortify your position as
     truthful and intelligent witness to the various
     occurrences, each of these gives you an additional right
     to assert what you know.                              [L90]
     It imposes upon you the sacred duty to instruct the
     public and prepare them for future possibilities by
     gradually opening their eyes to the truth.            [L91]

Sinnett straightens in his chair.
```

```
VISION — A FRONTIER CAMP — DAY

Dust, tents, mountains. A GENERAL'S TENT. SIR DONALD STEWART,
in full scarlet dress uniform, gold braid, a blue sash, rows of
medals, a white moustache, signs a DISPATCH at a field table.
He folds it and hands it to a rider.

A HARD CUT to Simla: the same dispatch read aloud to a room of
officials. Not one of them questions it. They nod. It is
Stewart's word. That is enough.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     The opportunity should not be lost through a lack of as
     great confidence in your own individual right of
     assertion as that of Sir Donald Stewart.              [L92]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     One witness of well known character outweighs the
     evidence of ten strangers; and if, there is any one in
     India who is respected for his trustworthiness it is —  [L93]

A pause on the dash. Sinnett looks down at the copy of THE
PIONEER on his desk, at the masthead, his paper.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …the Editor of the Pioneer.                           [L93]

He realises: the trusted witness is himself.
```

```
VISION — THE EMPTY TOMB — DAWN

Very quiet. A garden, pale grey light before sunrise. A rock
tomb, its great round STONE rolled aside.

At a distance, one WOMAN in a plain dark cloak, long auburn
hair, stands alone at the tomb's mouth, looking in. Utterly
still. We stay far from her, and never see what she sees.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (measured, without mockery)
     Remember that there was but one hysterical woman alleged
     to have been present at the pretended ascension, and
     that the phenomenon has never been corroborated by
     repetition.                                           [L94]

She turns and begins to run, out of frame, to tell someone.

The garden dissolves into centuries: a single candle lit in
the dark, then a few, then rows, then a cathedral of them,
thousands upon thousands of flames, all lit from that first
one.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Yet for nearly 2,000 years countless milliards have
     pinned their faith upon the testimony of that one woman —
     and she not over trustworthy.                         [L95]

The candles burn on.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett looks at the exhibits laid out on his desk: the cup,
the cigarette paper, the note, his notebook.

He sets the letter down, open at its last page.

He pulls a FRESH SHEET of paper toward him. Dips his pen. Holds
it above the page, ready.

CONTINUE INTO SCENE 10 (opens on L97).
```

## Visual and staging notes

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Character images** | Sinnett `alfred-percy-sinnett.jpg`, Blavatsky `helena-blavatsky.jpg`. Stewart `donald-stewart.jpg`: scarlet dress uniform, gold braid and epaulettes, blue sash, rows of medals and two stars, white moustache, balding grey hair. Mary Magdalene `mary-magdalene.jpg`: use only her face and long auburn hair. The image itself is a "penitent Magdalene" scene in a cave (bare shoulders, a skull, a crucifix, a straw mat), which doesn't suit the tomb at dawn. Re-render her in a plain dark cloak, or keep her far enough away that only the hair reads. Patience is off-screen (D3): a hand on the door frame and the edge of a shawl |
| **Stewart's uniform** | His image is in ceremonial dress, not field kit. For a frontier camp, either keep the dress uniform as a stylised "portrait come to life" or re-render him in field dress |
| **The campaign map** | The letter's own metaphor ("a man well acquainted with the strategy", "positions", "Enemies"). It appears twice: Sinnett pressing down an existing flag instead of reaching for a new one (L83), and the lone flag toppling (L88). Keep the map simple and handsome, like a general's field table |
| **The exhibits** | The cup, a torn cigarette paper and a folded note, laid side by side on the desk. The note's writing is always turned away, because its contents aren't known. The brooch isn't on the desk: it was Mrs. Hume's |
| **Blavatsky** | A single quiet shot, alone at a window, the mocking newspaper beside her. Her one appearance in this scene, so that "justice to the woman who is made to suffer for it" has a face |
| **"The Editor of the *Pioneer*"** | Hold the pause on L93's dash. The camera on the masthead of his paper, then on his face as he understands. It's the scene's turning point |
| **The tomb (D6)** | The most sensitive passage in the film. Stage it at a distance, in grey dawn light, with no mockery in image or music. The woman is shown as a sincere witness who runs to tell. The letter's words ("hysterical", "pretended", "not over trustworthy") are K.H.'s, voiced without emphasis. The candles multiplying from one flame show the letter's point (one testimony, carried for centuries) without endorsing or ridiculing the faith |
| **Palette** | Study: full daylight now, the brightest the room has been. Campaign map: parchment, green and brown hills, red and dark-blue flags. Breakfast tables: bright morning verandahs. Blavatsky's room: grey, cool window light. Frontier camp: dust gold and Stewart's scarlet. The tomb: grey-blue dawn. The candles: warm gold filling the dark |
| **Text in frame** | No readable text in the *Pioneer*, the dispatch or the note. The masthead is recognisable by shape |
| **Sound** | Study: pen, paper, the soft knock, Patience's footsteps going. Map: wind over hills, the flag's soft fall. Breakfast tables: teacups, a newspaper rustling, murmurs. Blavatsky's room: silence, a distant bird. Frontier camp: wind, horses, a pen on paper, then the dispatch read aloud. The tomb: birds before dawn, footsteps running away, then a choral hum swelling softly as the candles multiply. Study: silence, and a pen dipped in ink |
