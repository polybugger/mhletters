# Scene 01 — The Marvels of Simla

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | None. This is a prologue built from the analysis (§4, §9) and marked [context] throughout |
| **Settings** | S1 Simla hillside · S2 Hume's house · S3 Sinnett's study |
| **Characters** | Alfred Percy Sinnett, Helena Petrovna Blavatsky, Henry Steel Olcott, Allan Octavian Hume, picnic guests. Off-screen: Patience Sinnett, Mrs. Hume |
| **Feeling** | Curiosity, then excitement |
| **Weight** | M |

## Purpose

- Introduce the world: Simla in its season, a small British circle, the Himalaya on the horizon.
- Show the phenomena the letter refers to (the cup, the brooch, the cigarette papers) as the witnesses reportedly saw them, without explaining how they happened.
- Set up the two people the letter is about. Sinnett is an editor who has a story and wants proof big enough for London. Blavatsky ("the devoted woman") is the performer, already paying for it in ridicule.
- End with Sinnett proposing the newspaper test, so that scene 02 opens on the reply.

**Rules for this scene.** Everything here comes from the analysis's context, not the letter, so every line is [dramatized]. The events are kept general, as the analysis flags their details as approximate. Nothing here should contradict what the letter later says: phenomena were produced "for yourself and friends" (L84), a case appeared "under notice in the *Pioneer*" (L88), "the skeptics are quite satisfied with their present hypothesis for the production of the brooch" (L87), and Sinnett "originated the idea" of the test (L17).

## Beat outline

1. **Title card.** Simla, early October 1880.
2. **The picnic.** A cup is missing. Blavatsky points to the ground, and a cup and saucer are dug up from the hillside. The guests are amazed; one skeptic smiles.
3. **The cigarette papers.** A small, quieter marvel by the fire. Blavatsky's cigarette. Sinnett takes notes.
4. **The brooch at Hume's.** After dinner a lost brooch turns up. Hume is polite and unconvinced.
5. **The study, late night.** Sinnett is energised. His *Pioneer* report sits on the desk, and a skeptical newspaper lies beside it. He decides one decisive proof would settle everything.
6. **The proposal.** He writes to the Masters: make a newspaper appear far from where it was printed on the day it is published.
7. **Blavatsky's warning.** She takes the letter and warns him. He insists. She goes. The lamp gutters. Hold on the empty doorway, then cut to black.

## Script

```
FADE IN:

TITLE CARD: "Simla. The summer capital of British India. Early October, 1880."
```

```
EXT. HILLSIDE ABOVE SIMLA — DAY

Deodar pines on a steep slope. Far off, the white wall of the
Himalaya. Below, the roofs of Simla cling to the ridge.

A PICNIC on rugs under the trees. Servants unpack hampers. A
dozen English men and women in autumn tweeds and bonnets.

ALFRED PERCY SINNETT (40s, editor, bald crown, grey beard,
dark frock coat) stands apart, notebook in hand, watching
everything like a man who writes for a living.

HELENA PETROVNA BLAVATSKY (late 40s, curly grey hair, black
dress, a long chain, rings on her fingers) sits heavily on a
folding chair, a cigarette between her fingers, bored by the
English.

HENRY STEEL OLCOTT (late 40s, long white hair and beard, dark
suit) sits beside her with a cup in his hand.

A SERVANT lays out the tea things. Counts. Counts again.
Glances at a newcomer who joined the party on the road.

                    SERVANT
          (low, to a guest)
     One cup short, memsahib.

                    GUEST (WOMAN)
          (laughing)
     Then someone must go without. Madame, I suppose you
     could not conjure us one?

Laughter. Blavatsky does not laugh. She looks at the guest a
moment too long. Then, slowly, she points at the ground a few
paces off, between the roots of a tree.

                    BLAVATSKY
     Dig. There.

A pause. Someone snorts. A YOUNG OFFICER grins, takes a knife
from the hamper and kneels, playing along.

He scrapes at the earth. Roots. Stones. More earth.

His knife strikes something hard. He stops grinning.

He brushes the soil away. White china. He lifts out a CUP, then
a SAUCER, packed in earth. He holds them up beside the others.

They match the set.

Silence on the hillside. Then everyone speaks at once.

                    GUEST (WOMAN)
     It can't be. It cannot be.

                    SKEPTICAL GUEST (MAN)
          (dry, to his neighbour)
     A clever servant and a spade the night before.

Sinnett has not looked away from the cup. He writes fast.

                    SINNETT
          (to Olcott, quietly)
     You saw her. She never left that chair.

                    OLCOTT
          (calmly, as if it were nothing)
     She rarely needs to.

On Blavatsky: she draws on her cigarette and looks out at the
mountains, as if none of this concerned her.
```

```
EXT. HILLSIDE — LATER — DUSK

A small fire. The party has thinned. A few guests gather close.

Blavatsky tears the corner from a cigarette paper and hands it
to a guest to hold. A quiet exchange we cannot hear.

Moments later the guest gasps, staring at something in her
palm. The others lean in. We do not see what she sees.

Sinnett sits a little apart, writing by firelight, filling
page after page.

                    PATIENCE SINNETT (O.S.)
          (warm, from the dark)
     Alfred, you will run out of paper before she runs out
     of wonders.

He smiles without looking up. Her hand rests briefly on his
shoulder, then is gone.
```

```
INT. HUME'S HOUSE — DRAWING ROOM — NIGHT

Lamplight, heavy furniture, a piano, French windows open on a
dark garden. Dinner is over. Guests take coffee.

ALLAN OCTAVIAN HUME (50s, grey hair and beard, light frock coat,
spotted cravat) stands at the mantelpiece, a host at ease in
his own house.

A murmur at the French windows. Guests come in from the garden,
excited. A WOMAN'S HAND (MRS. HUME, O.S.) holds a small
BROOCH up to the lamp. It catches the light.

                    MRS. HUME (O.S.)
          (breathless)
     I lost it years ago. Years.

The room crowds around her, out of frame. Blavatsky stays in
her chair, smoking. Hume does not move from the mantelpiece.
He watches Blavatsky, not the brooch.

Sinnett comes to stand beside him.

                    SINNETT
     Well, Hume?

                    HUME
          (pleasantly)
     Well. A charming evening, and a lady made very happy.

                    SINNETT
     That is not an answer.

                    HUME
     It is the only one I can give at my own dinner table.
          (beat)
     Ask me what I can prove, and I shall tell you: nothing,
     yet. Ask me what a jury of sensible men would say, and
     you know it already.

                    SINNETT
     Then we must give the sensible men something they cannot
     explain away.

Hume raises his cup an inch, half toast, half challenge.

Across the room, Blavatsky looks up and meets Sinnett's eye.
She has heard every word.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — NIGHT

Later. A narrow, cluttered room. Bookshelves, a desk heaped with
galley proofs, a lamp. Mist presses at the window; somewhere
below, a dog barks in the dark town.

On the desk: a copy of THE PIONEER, folded open at his own report
of the Simla wonders. Beside it, another paper, folded open at
a mocking reply. He has underlined a line in it twice.

Sinnett paces. Picks up the cup from the picnic (he has kept
it). Sets it down by the newspapers. Looks at the three objects
together: the marvel, his report, the mockery.

                    SINNETT
          (to himself)
     One case. One story. And they laugh at it over breakfast.

He sits. Pulls a clean sheet toward him. Dips his pen.

INSERT: his hand writing. We catch only fragments: "...a test
which no reasonable man could dispute..." "...a newspaper,
printed in London, here in Simla on the very day of its
publication..."

                    SINNETT (V.O.)
          (as he writes, gathering conviction)
     Give me that, and I will put it before the world. Half
     London would be converted if you could deliver them a
     Pioneer on its day of publication.

He sets down the pen. Reads it over. Pleased with himself.

A KNOCK. Blavatsky in the doorway, shawl around her shoulders,
a candle in her hand.

                    BLAVATSKY
     You are writing to them.

                    SINNETT
     I am writing to whoever reads what I give you.
          (holds up the letter)
     Will you send it?

She comes in. Takes the letter. Does not read it. Weighs it in
her hand.

                    BLAVATSKY
     They do not do tricks for editors, my dear Mr. Sinnett.

                    SINNETT
     It is not a trick if it is true. That is the whole point.

                    BLAVATSKY
          (tired, almost tender)
     The whole point. Yes.
          (beat)
     You think the world is waiting for proof. You have not
     seen what the world does with it.

                    SINNETT
     Then let them tell me so themselves.

A long look between them. She tucks the letter into her shawl.

                    BLAVATSKY
     They will.

She goes. The candle's glow leaves with her.

Sinnett alone. The lamp gutters, steadies.

On the desk, the dug-up cup sits beside THE PIONEER, earth
still in the rim.

HOLD on the empty doorway.

CUT TO BLACK.
```
