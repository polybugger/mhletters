# Scene 04 — The Modern Hookeses

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | L43–L48 |
| **Settings** | S9 Hooke's study · a Victorian laboratory (vision) · S3 Sinnett's study |
| **Characters** | Alfred Percy Sinnett, Koot' Hoomi (V.O.). Vision figures, silent: Robert Hooke, Isaac Newton, Victorian men of science |
| **Feeling** | Irony |
| **Weight** | S |

## Purpose

- A short, dry interlude after scene 03's wonder. K.H. explains why science won't accept *Akás*: it admits only facts that pass through its own methods. It would rather classify a thing than understand it.
- Use Hooke against Newton as the letter's single example. A learned man who catalogues surfaces opposes a greater discovery.
- Close the letter's argument about science with "So far for Science". That line is the hinge into scene 05, the letter's turn to human nature and the escalation trap.

**Rules for this scene.** K.H. lines are word for word with line numbers, and "…" marks a cut. Hooke, Newton and the men of science are silent and act out only what the letter says. Sinnett doesn't speak. The history is played as the letter tells it.

## Beat outline

1. **Their own property.** The dug-up cup from Sinnett's desk is carried into a Victorian laboratory. Nobody will touch it until it has passed through their instruments and been stamped as theirs.
2. **Hooke's study.** Hooke at his microscope, drawing the plates of *Micrographia*: the flea, the fly's eye. Surfaces, enlarged and perfect.
3. **Newton's light.** Newton sets a prism on Hooke's table and splits a beam of light into colour. Hooke files it away in a drawer and goes back to his flea.
4. **The modern Hookeses.** Hooke's single drawer multiplies into a vast Victorian hall of cabinets, with rows of men labelling and filing.
5. **True or plausible.** A thread of the blue *Akás* light from scene 03 drifts into the hall. They ignore it and file a tidy card instead.
6. **"So far for Science."** Back in the study, Sinnett lays a page aside. The letter turns from science to people.

## Script

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett holds the dug-up CUP, turning it in the light.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     We doubt not but the men of your science are open to
     conviction;                                           [L43]
```

```
VISION — A VICTORIAN LABORATORY

Gaslight on brass and glass. The same CUP, earth in its rim, is
set on the counter of a laboratory by an unseen hand.

Behind the counter, MEN OF SCIENCE in frock coats look at it
over their spectacles. Nobody picks it up.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …yet facts must be first demonstrated to them, they
     must first have become their own property,            [L43]

A clerk slides the cup onto a tray. It travels down a line of
INSTRUMENTS: a balance, a microscope, a spectroscope, a
callipered stand. At each one a man peers, measures and writes.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …have proved amenable to their own modes of
     investigation,                                        [L43]

At the end of the line a heavy STAMP comes down on a ledger
beside the cup. Only then do the men nod and lean in with
interest.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …before you find them ready to admit them as facts.   [L43]
```

```
INT. HOOKE'S STUDY — LONDON — DAY (17TH CENTURY)

A tall window onto green hills. A column, a red-draped table,
an armillary sphere.

ROBERT HOOKE (young, long brown hair, brown cloak, lace
cravat) bends over a brass MICROSCOPE. Beside it, great copper
PLATES of engravings: a FLEA the size of a dog, a FLY'S EYE
like a honeycomb, the grain of cork.

He draws, absorbed, delighted, with a pair of dividers in one
hand. Every hair on the flea's leg, exact.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     If you but look into the Preface to the "Micrographia"
     you will find in Hooke's suggestions that the intimate
     relations of objects were of less account in his eyes
     than their external operation on the senses —         [L44]

A KNOCK. Hooke looks up, irritated.

ISAAC NEWTON (silver-white hair, dark brown robe) stands at the
table. Without a word he sets down a glass PRISM, angles it to
the window, and a beam of white light breaks into a SPECTRUM
across Hooke's engravings: red, orange, gold, green, blue,
violet, a rainbow laid over the flea.

Newton waits.

Hooke looks at the colours. Then, briskly, he closes the
shutter halfway. The spectrum vanishes. He opens a DRAWER in
his cabinet, slides the prism inside, shuts it, and turns back
to his microscope.

Newton stands very still.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …and Newton's fine discoveries found in him their
     greatest opponent.                                    [L44]
```

```
VISION — THE HALL OF CABINETS

The camera pulls back from Hooke's drawer, and the drawer
multiplies. Ten drawers, a hundred, a wall of them. The
seventeenth-century study becomes a VAST VICTORIAN HALL, iron
and glass, lined to the roof with specimen cabinets.

On ladders and at long desks, ROWS OF MEN in frock coats write
labels, sort cards, pin specimens and close drawers. A sea of
neat hands, all busy.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (dry)
     The modern Hookeses are many.                         [L45]
     Like this learned but ignorant man of old your modern
     men of science are less anxious to suggest a physical
     connexion of facts which might unlock for them many an
     occult force in nature, as to provide a convenient
     "classification of scientific experiments";           [L46]
```

```
VISION — THE HALL OF CABINETS — CONTINUOUS

A thread of pale BLUE LIGHT, the Akás glow from the cave,
drifts in through a high window and winds slowly between the
desks, brushing past the men's sleeves.

One man glances at it. Hesitates. Looks down at his card,
writes a tidy label, files the card in a drawer and closes it.
The blue thread drifts on, unclaimed, and out through the far
wall.

On the man's desk: a small brass BALANCE. In one pan, nothing
we can see, only a faint shimmer. In the other, the neat white
card. The balance tips toward the card.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …so that the most essential quality of an hypothesis is
     not that it should be true but only plausible — in their
     opinion.                                              [L46]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett sets the cup back on the desk, carefully, beside THE
PIONEER.

He lays one page of the letter face down on the pile of read
pages, and lifts the next.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (a breath, a shift of tone)
     So far for Science — as much as we know of it.        [L48]

A cloud crosses the sun. The room dims.

CONTINUE INTO SCENE 05 (opens on L49).
```

## Visual and staging notes

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Character images** | Sinnett `alfred-percy-sinnett.jpg`. Hooke `robert-hooke.jpg`: young, long brown wavy hair, brown cloak over a white shirt, lace cravat, dividers in hand, an armillary sphere and a red-draped table, a window onto green hills. Build his study from that image. Newton `isaac-newton.jpg`: long silver-white wavy hair, dark brown robe, a steady sideways look. K.H. is voice only. The men of science have no images and stay in rows, backs and hands, no faces |
| **Tone** | The letter's irony, played straight. No mugging, and no villain music. Hooke isn't a fool: he's brilliant and absorbed in the wrong thing. The joke is the drawer |
| **The key image** | The spectrum laid across the flea, then cut off by a half-closed shutter. That's the whole of L44 in one gesture. Let the rainbow be the most colourful thing in the scene |
| **Match cuts** | The cup in Sinnett's hand to the same cup on the laboratory counter. Hooke's single drawer multiplying into the hall of cabinets. The blue thread is the *Akás* light from scene 03, unclaimed |
| **The balance** | One pan holds only a shimmer (the true), the other a white card (the plausible). The balance tips toward the card. Keep the pans unlabelled. The image works without words |
| **Palette** | Laboratory: gaslight amber and brass. Hooke's study: the colours of his image (brown, cream, deep red, green hills). The spectrum is the only full-colour moment. The hall of cabinets: grey iron, white cards, rows of black coats. The blue thread is the one cool colour. Study: daylight dimming at the end, as the tone darkens into scene 05 |
| **Text in frame** | No readable labels, ledgers or card text. Shapes only |
| **Length** | Short. Every vision is one clear action. This scene is a breath between scene 03's wonder and scene 05's peak |
| **Sound** | Laboratory: instruments clicking, a scratching pen, the heavy thud of the stamp. Hooke's study: birdsong outside, the dividers' tick on copper, a knock, then the soft clap of the shutter and the drawer sliding shut. Hall of cabinets: a rising murmur of thousands of drawers opening and closing like a tide. Study: the room goes quiet as the cloud passes |
