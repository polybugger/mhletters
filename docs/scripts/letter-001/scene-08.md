# Scene 08 — At Mr. Hume's

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | L77–L79 |
| **Settings** | S3 Sinnett's study · S2 Hume's house (the library, late night) · S14 the adepts' world |
| **Characters** | Alfred Percy Sinnett, Allan Octavian Hume, Koot' Hoomi (**first full appearance**), Morya (optional, silent) |
| **Feeling** | Intimacy, humility |
| **Weight** | M |

## Purpose

- The letter turns personal. After the long argument it softens: "Many of your suggestions are highly reasonable".
- Show the conversation at Hume's that K.H. says he "listened attentively" to. These are the arguments he calls "perfect from the standpoint of exoteric wisdom".
- **Reveal K.H.** For the first time we see his face, in his own world, listening across the distance. The voice we've heard since scene 02 finally has a face.
- End on humility. The adepts can create causes "at our will" but can't control their consequences, which "thus become our masters". That's why their acts can look "unwise, if not actually foolish".

**Rules for this scene.** K.H. lines are word for word with line numbers, and "…" marks a cut. The conversation at Hume's is [dramatized]. The letter says only that it took place, that Sinnett made "suggestions" there, and that Hume's arguments were "perfect from the standpoint of exoteric wisdom". So the dialogue gives Hume reasonable, worldly arguments of that kind, consistent with his lines in scene 01, and adds no new historical facts. K.H. and Morya don't speak aloud. K.H.'s words stay in voice-over.

## Beat outline

1. **Reasonable suggestions.** Sinnett reads, and his face eases for the first time since scene 02.
2. **Hume's library, late at night.** After the guests have gone, Hume and Sinnett by the fire. Hume argues lucidly: proof must be on terms reasonable men set, and secrecy only breeds suspicion. Sinnett agrees with every point.
3. **The curtain.** A curtain at the window stirs, though the window is shut.
4. **The reveal.** Across the distance, in a wood-panelled room with snow at the window, K.H. sits listening. We see his face. Morya stands beyond him in the doorway, silent.
5. **Perfect, from one standpoint.** K.H. listens to Hume with real respect.
6. **The drop of ink.** K.H. lets a single drop of ink fall into a glass of water. It spreads in tendrils, branching faster and wider than any hand could guide. Causes and consequences.
7. **Both you and he.** Cut between Sinnett in his study and Hume alone in his library, each holding a part of the same idea.
8. **Unwise, if not foolish.** K.H. looks up from the clouded glass with a faint, rueful smile. Morya nods once and withdraws.

## Script

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett turns to the next page. Braced for more.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (warmer)
     Many of your suggestions are highly reasonable and will
     be attended to.                                       [L77]

Sinnett blinks. For the first time since the envelope opened,
his face eases.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     I listened attentively to the conversation which took
     place at Mr. Hume's.                                  [L78]

Sinnett looks up sharply, as if someone had spoken behind him.
```

```
INT. HUME'S HOUSE — LIBRARY — NIGHT (SOME DAYS EARLIER)

After the dinner of scene 01. The guests have gone. A smaller
room than the drawing room: books to the ceiling, a dying fire,
two armchairs, a decanter, cigar smoke.

HUME in one chair, coat unbuttoned, at his ease and at his
sharpest. SINNETT opposite, leaning forward.

                    HUME [dramatized]
     I will grant you the brooch. I will grant you the cup.
     I will grant you that I cannot explain either. And I
     will tell you what any court in England would say of
     both: not proven.

                    SINNETT [dramatized]
     Because the conditions were not theirs to set.

                    HUME [dramatized]
     Precisely. Proof is not a gift, Sinnett. It is a
     bargain. The man who claims a thing must meet the doubter
     on the doubter's ground. If these Brothers of yours can
     do what she says, let them do it where we choose, when
     we choose, before witnesses we choose.

                    SINNETT [dramatized]
     And if they refuse?

                    HUME [dramatized]
          (gently)
     Then sensible men will draw the sensible conclusion.
          (beat)
     Secrecy is the natural home of humbug. I don't say
     that is what this is. I say that is what the world will
     say. And the world will be reasonable to say it.

Sinnett nods slowly. Every word is what he has thought
himself.

                    SINNETT [dramatized]
     Then I shall ask them for exactly that.

                    HUME [dramatized]
     Ask them for something no one could fake. And let it be
     public.

Hume lifts his glass. Sinnett lifts his. The fire settles.

Behind them, at the tall WINDOW, the heavy CURTAIN stirs, a
slow ripple from hem to rail.

The window is shut. Neither man notices.
```

```
INT. THE ADEPTS' WORLD — K.H.'S ROOM — NIGHT

The ripple of the curtain becomes the ripple of a different
curtain, at a different window, a world away.

A small wood-panelled ROOM. An oil lamp. A clock on a shelf, a
row of old books. At the window, deep snow and mountains under
stars.

In a chair by the lamp, still, listening, his head a little
inclined as if to a voice in the next room:

KOOT' HOOMI.

For the first time, we see his face. Long dark wavy hair. A
full dark beard. A fur cap. A dark robe with a fur collar.
Green-grey eyes, steady and kind, focused on something far
away.

Faintly, as if from very far off, we hear HUME'S VOICE:

                    HUME (O.S., distant) [dramatized]
     …secrecy is the natural home of humbug…

K.H. listens. He does not smile at it, or frown. He considers
it, the way one considers a good move by a respected opponent.

In the doorway behind him, half in shadow: MORYA, taller, in a
white turban and robe, a long dark beard, arms folded. He
listens too.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     His arguments are perfect from the standpoint of
     exoteric wisdom.                                      [L78]
```

```
INT. K.H.'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS

K.H. turns to his desk. On it: the LETTER we have watched
Sinnett read, half-written, in his flowing hand. A pen. An
inkwell. A small GLASS of clear water.

He dips the pen. Holds it over the glass of water.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     But, when the time comes and he is allowed to have a full
     glimpse into the world of esoterism, with its laws based
     upon mathematically correct calculations of the future —  [L79]

He lets a single DROP of ink fall from the nib.

Close on the GLASS: the drop strikes the water and blooms into
a dark tendril, then two, then many, branching, curling,
spreading through the glass, faster and wider than any hand
could guide, until the whole glass is a living cloud of ink.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …the necessary results of the causes which we are always
     at liberty to create and shape at our will but are as
     unable to control their consequences which thus become
     our masters —                                         [L79]

K.H. watches it spread. He doesn't try to stop it. He can't.
```

```
INTERCUT — SINNETT'S STUDY / HUME'S LIBRARY — DAY

SINNETT at his desk with the letter, very still.

HUME, alone in his library by daylight, a book open on his
knee, gazing out of the window at the mountains as if he'd
heard his name.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …then only will, both you and he understand            [L79]

Two men, two rooms, the same mountains beyond both windows.
```

```
INT. K.H.'S ROOM — NIGHT

K.H. looks up from the clouded glass. A faint, rueful smile,
the first we have seen.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …why to the uninitiated our acts must seem often unwise,
     if not actually foolish.                              [L79]

In the doorway, Morya inclines his head once, as if agreeing
with something long understood between them, and withdraws into
the dark.

K.H. turns back to the letter on his desk, dips his pen in
fresh ink, and writes on.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett lowers the page. He looks at the window for a long
moment. Then he does something he hasn't done all morning: he
picks up his own PEN.

He doesn't write yet. He holds it.

CONTINUE INTO SCENE 09 (opens on L81).
```

## Visual and staging notes

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Character images** | K.H. `koot-hoomi.jpg`, **full face for the first time**. Build his room from his image: wood panelling, a window with soft light, an oil lamp, a mantel clock, a shelf of books, the fur cap and fur-collared dark robe. Morya `morya.jpg`: white turban and robe, long dark beard, pale eyes. His image is drawn on aged paper, so keep him in shadow and soft focus to blend him into the room. Hume `allan-octavian-hume.jpg`, Sinnett `alfred-percy-sinnett.jpg` |
| **The reveal** | The emotional centre of the scene, so make it quiet, not grand. Hold on K.H.'s face listening, with no music swell. The audience has waited since scene 02 to see him. Let them look. His expression is respect for Hume, not superiority |
| **Hume's arguments** | Written to be genuinely good. Proof on the doubter's terms, and secrecy reading as humbug. That's what makes K.H.'s "perfect from the standpoint of exoteric wisdom" generous rather than dismissive. Hume should win the room |
| **The curtain** | The stirring curtain at Hume's (window shut) is the only sign of K.H.'s listening there. It match-cuts to the curtain in K.H.'s room, bridging the two worlds |
| **The ink drop** | The key image. One drop in clear water, filmed close and slowly, blooming into branching tendrils that fill the glass. It shows "causes which we are … at liberty to create" and consequences that "become our masters" without any explanation. K.H. watches; he doesn't intervene |
| **Morya** | Optional (decision D5). Silent, in the doorway, arms folded. Two appearances: listening, then the single nod and withdrawal. If cut, nothing else changes |
| **Intercut** | Sinnett and Hume each alone, the same mountains behind both windows. It's the letter's "both you and he". Brief, two shots |
| **Sinnett's pen** | He picks it up at the end without writing. It sets up scene 09, where the letter gives him his orders and he starts writing for real in scene 10 |
| **Palette** | Study: daylight. Hume's library: deep amber firelight, cigar haze, dark leather. K.H.'s room: warm lamplight inside, blue snow light outside, the warmest and most intimate room in the film. The ink in water is the only black, spreading through clear light |
| **Text in frame** | The letter on K.H.'s desk is visible as flowing handwriting, not readable |
| **Sound** | Study: a clock, a page turning, a sudden silence on "Mr. Hume's". Hume's library: fire, a decanter, the clink of glasses, the curtain's whisper at the end. K.H.'s room: deep quiet, a mantel clock, the lamp's faint hiss, Hume's voice very far off as if through a wall. The ink drop: a tiny sound, then silence while it spreads. One soft sustained tone under the intercut. Study: birdsong |
