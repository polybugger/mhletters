# Scene 02 — Unthinkable

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | L11–L27 |
| **Settings** | S3 Sinnett's study · S4 Hyde Park, London (imagined) · a vision space for "the door" |
| **Characters** | Alfred Percy Sinnett, Koot' Hoomi (V.O. and glimpses), Helena Petrovna Blavatsky (brief, then in the vision) |
| **Feeling** | Shock |
| **Weight** | M |

## Purpose

- The reply arrives, and the refusal lands in the first line. Scene 01's excitement turns to shock.
- Establish K.H.'s voice: calm, exact, faintly ironic, never angry.
- Show both outcomes of the test, as the letter describes them. If it succeeds and is believed, Sinnett is in danger. If it isn't believed, his name is ruined. Either way, Blavatsky is in danger.
- Turn Sinnett from a man asking for proof into a man being taught. He keeps reading into scene 03.

**Rules for this scene.** Every K.H. line is quoted exactly from the letter and tagged with its line number. A "…" marks a cut within a line. Sinnett's few lines are [dramatized] reactions. The letter's own words carry the scene.

## Beat outline

1. **Title card.** About 15 October 1880.
2. **The letter arrives.** Grey morning in the study. Blavatsky sets an envelope on the desk without a word and goes.
3. **The refusal.** Sinnett opens it eagerly. K.H.'s voice begins, and the first sentence is a refusal: "it is unthinkable."
4. **The world unprepared.** Two quick visions: men of science baffled, and a crowd falling to its knees at a "miracle".
5. **The door.** Blavatsky "rushes into the wide open door leading to notoriety". The light beyond it turns cold, and the door becomes a trap.
6. **The shadow.** K.H. turns from Sinnett to the world: "Madmen are they…" An "inexorable shadow" follows every innovation, and here it would be blamed on "dark agencies".
7. **Hyde Park, version one.** In London, Sinnett holds up a *Pioneer* on its day of publication. The crowd believes him, and turns on him.
8. **Hyde Park, version two.** The same moment, but nobody believes. Laughter, turned backs, his good name gone.
9. **Back in the study.** Sinnett lowers the letter, shaken. He turns the page and reads on, into scene 03.

## Script

```
TITLE CARD: "About October 15th, 1880."
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — MORNING

Grey light. The mist of the night before has thinned to drizzle
on the window. The lamp is out.

On the desk, exactly as we left them: the dug-up CUP, THE
PIONEER, the mocking newspaper.

Sinnett at the desk in shirtsleeves, unshaven, working through
proofs without reading them.

The door opens. BLAVATSKY, in black, shawl around her, crosses
the room without a word and sets an ENVELOPE on the desk, square
in front of him. Thick paper. No stamp.

She looks at him a moment. Whatever she knows, she does not
say it. She goes.

Sinnett stares at the envelope. Then tears it open like a man
opening a telegram he has been waiting for.

He unfolds the pages. Close on the handwriting, flowing and
even.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (calm, unhurried, almost gentle)
     Esteemed Brother and Friend,                          [L11]

Sinnett half smiles at the greeting.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Precisely because the test of the London newspaper
     would close the mouths of the skeptics — it is
     unthinkable.                                          [L13]

The smile goes. He reads the line again.

                    SINNETT
          (under his breath) [dramatized]
     Because it would work?

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     See it in what light you will — the world is yet in
     its first stage of disenthralment if not development,
     hence — unprepared.                                   [L14]
```

```
VISION — A LECTURE HALL — NIGHT

A crowded Victorian lecture theatre, gaslit. On the
demonstration bench, under glass: a NEWSPAPER, impossibly
present. Bearded MEN OF SCIENCE lean over it with magnifying
glasses and callipers. One turns it over. Another shakes his
head. None of them can explain it.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Very true, we work by natural not supernatural means
     and laws.                                             [L15]
     But, as on the one hand Science would find itself
     unable (in its present state) to account for the
     wonders given in its name,                            [L16]
```

```
VISION — A VILLAGE SQUARE — DAY

The same newspaper, held up at the centre of a CROWD. Faces of
every kind: awe, terror. People fall to their knees. A woman
crosses herself. A man covers his eyes. Someone cries out and
runs.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …and on the other the ignorant masses would still be
     left to view the phenomenon in the light of a miracle;
     everyone who would thus be made a witness to the
     occurrence would be thrown off his balance and the
     results would be deplorable.                          [L16]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — MORNING

Sinnett, very still.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Believe me, it would be so — especially for yourself
     who originated the idea,                              [L17]

Sinnett's eyes go to the cup on the desk, the newspapers. His
idea.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …and the devoted woman who so foolishly rushes into the
     wide open door leading to notoriety.                  [L17]
```

```
VISION — THE DOOR

Darkness. A tall DOOR stands alone, wide open. Through it,
blinding light, applause, the flash and pop of magnesium,
voices calling her name.

BLAVATSKY hurries toward it, shawl slipping, not looking back,
drawn to the noise. Her chain swings.

Behind her, a hand (SINNETT'S, we know the cuff) holds the door
open for her.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     This door, though opened by so friendly a hand as
     yours, would prove very soon a trap — and a fatal one
     indeed for her.                                       [L18]

She crosses the threshold. The light turns cold. The applause
becomes a roar, then jeering. The door frame narrows behind
her like a closing iron gate.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     And such is not surely your object?                   [L19]

SNAP to black.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — MORNING

Sinnett lets out a breath he has been holding. He wipes his
face with one hand.

                    SINNETT
          (quietly) [dramatized]
     No. No, it is not.

He reads on. K.H.'s voice turns from Sinnett to the world, a
little colder.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Madmen are they, who, speculating but upon the present,
     wilfully shut their eyes to the past when made already
     to remain naturally blind to the future!              [L21]
     Far be it from me, to number you with the latter —
     therefore will I endeavour to explain.                [L22]

GLIMPSE — K.H.

Snow light through a small high window. A DESK. A HAND in a
dark fur-trimmed sleeve, writing steadily. We see the sleeve,
the hand, the moving pen. Never the face. A strand of long
dark hair falls across the page and is brushed back.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Were we to accede to your desires know you really what
     consequences would follow in the trail of success?    [L23]
     The inexorable shadow which follows all human
     innovations moves on, yet few are they, who are ever
     conscious of its approach and dangers.                [L24]

On the study wall behind Sinnett, the window frame's SHADOW
lengthens across the books as a cloud passes, long and slow.
```

```
VISION — A CITY OF SHADOWS

The newspaper again, glowing, carried through a dark street.
Behind it a SHADOW grows: horned, clawed, enormous, the shape
frightened people give to what they don't understand. Shutters
slam. Faces at windows recoil.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     What are then to expect they, who would offer the world
     an innovation which, owing to human ignorance, if
     believed in, will surely be attributed to those dark
     agencies the two-thirds of humanity believe in and
     dread as yet?                                         [L25]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — MORNING

Sinnett almost laughs: the letter quotes him to himself.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
          (a trace of dry humour)
     You say — half London would be converted if you could
     deliver them a Pioneer on its day of publication.     [L26]
```

```
VISION — HYDE PARK, LONDON — DAY (VERSION ONE: BELIEVED)

Grey London sky. Bare plane trees. A SPEAKER'S CROWD gathered
on the grass.

SINNETT stands on a crate, holding up a copy of THE PIONEER,
fresh, the ink barely dry. He is triumphant. The crowd presses
closer to see the date.

Murmurs. Then shouting. Faces twist. It is not wonder. It is
fear, and then rage. Someone tears the paper from his hand. He
is pulled off the crate. The crowd closes over him.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     I beg to say that if the people believed the thing true
     they would kill you before you could make the round of
     Hyde Park;                                            [L27]

A dropped PIONEER, trampled into the mud.
```

```
VISION — HYDE PARK — DAY (VERSION TWO: NOT BELIEVED)

REWIND to the same moment. Sinnett on the crate, paper held
high.

This time the crowd laughs. Someone shouts "Humbug!" A
gentleman in a top hat shakes his head and walks away. Others
follow. A newsboy mimes a conjuror pulling a rabbit from a hat.

Sinnett is left on the crate, arm still raised, the paper
drooping, alone on the grass.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …if it were not believed true, — the least that could
     happen would be the loss of your reputation and good
     name, — for propagating such ideas.                   [L27]
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — MORNING

Sinnett sets the letter down on the desk. He looks at THE
PIONEER beside it: his paper, his name.

A long beat. The drizzle has stopped. Thin sunlight edges the
window.

He picks the letter up again, turns the page, and reads on.

CONTINUE INTO SCENE 03 (opens on L29).
```

## Visual and staging notes

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Character images** | Sinnett `alfred-percy-sinnett.jpg`, Blavatsky `helena-blavatsky.jpg`, K.H. `koot-hoomi.jpg`, shown only as hand, sleeve and hair, never the face in this scene. Men of science, crowds and the newsboy have no images and stay in silhouette or soft focus |
| **K.H.'s presence** | Voice first, then a glimpse of the hand writing, matching the fur-trimmed dark robe and long dark hair of his image. Saving his face for later gives scene 08 ("At Mr. Hume's") its reveal |
| **K.H.'s voice** | Low, warm, unhurried. Precise rather than mystical. A faint dry humour on L26 ("You say — half London…"), a cooler edge on L21 ("Madmen are they…"). Never angry |
| **Two palettes** | The study: grey morning slowly warming to thin sun at the end, so his mood lifts slightly as he reads on. The visions: desaturated and high-contrast, with one strong colour each. Gaslight amber in the lecture hall, harsh white through the door, ink black for the shadow city, London grey for Hyde Park |
| **The door** | The scene's key image. It starts as a real door held by Sinnett's hand (his cuff matches his coat), and the frame narrows into iron bars. The cut to black on L19 should feel like a slam |
| **Hyde Park, two versions** | Same framing, same opening frame, then diverge. Version one ends on the trampled *Pioneer* in mud. Version two ends on Sinnett alone with his arm raised. The "rewind" can be a visible reverse (crowd sucked backward) or a hard reset to the opening frame |
| **Recurring props** | The dug-up cup and *The Pioneer* from scene 01 stay on the desk. The trampled *Pioneer* in Hyde Park echoes the real copy on his desk. Cut from the mud to the clean copy at the end |
| **Text in frame** | Keep the handwriting legible only in fragments. The spoken words carry the content. Don't show a readable newspaper date |
| **Transitions** | Study to vision as a slow push-in on the letter's handwriting, which dissolves into each vision. Vision to study as a hard cut back to Sinnett's face. The final beat continues without a break into scene 03, which opens on L29 |
| **Sound** | Drizzle, the tear of the envelope, paper unfolding, then near-silence under K.H.'s voice. Visions: murmur to roar in the village square, applause to jeers at the door, the crowd's rage in version one, scattered laughter in version two. Back in the study, birdsong as the sun comes out |
