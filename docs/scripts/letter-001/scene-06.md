# Scene 06 — More Than One Martyr

| | |
|---|---|
| **Letter lines** | L62–L68 |
| **Settings** | S3 Sinnett's study (bookends) · S11 the gallery of martyrs and the scenes behind its frames: Athens, Samos, Rome, an English court and prison, Elizabeth's court |
| **Characters** | Alfred Percy Sinnett (bookends), Koot' Hoomi (V.O.). Vision figures, mostly silent: Socrates, Aristarchus of Samos, Nicolaus Copernicus, Galileo Galilei, Robert Recorde, Edward VI, William Gilbert, Elizabeth I, Paracelsus, Cornelius Agrippa, John Dee, Francis Bacon. Allegory: Science, personified as a woman, as the letter does ("her face", "her sacrilegious hand") |
| **Feeling** | Solemn |
| **Weight** | L |

## Purpose

- After the peak's noise and silence, a slow, grave walk. The letter's point: the world has always punished people who were ahead of it, and will do so again.
- Make the history personal: one face at a time, each punished or dismissed by science, Church or colleagues, as the letter tells it.
- Keep *Akás* in view. Science "laid her sacrilegious hand" on "The Heavenly White Virgin (*Akás*)", so the blue light returns and is dimmed.
- End on Bacon, the "Father of Inductive Philosophy", judging them all. The founder of modern science dismisses the men the letter honours.

**Rules for this scene.** K.H. lines are word for word with line numbers, and "…" marks a cut. Historical figures are silent except for words the letter itself quotes:
- Aristarchus: "affirmeth that the Earth moveth circularly about her own centre"
- Recorde's colleagues: "vain phantasies"
- Bacon: "Alchemicians of the Fantastic philosophy"

Science is an allegorical figure, built from the letter's own personification. The history is played as the letter tells it. Sinnett is silent.

## Beat outline

1. **The gallery.** Sinnett's study dissolves into a long, dim gallery hung with framed portraits. Martyrs past, and empty frames waiting for martyrs to come.
2. **Socrates.** The first frame opens: Athens, the cup of hemlock. "More than one Socrates…"
3. **The turned face.** A tall robed woman, Science, walks the gallery with a lamp. At the frames of Aristarchus and Copernicus she turns her face away.
4. **Galileo.** Before a Church tribunal. Behind him, the shadow of an altar fire: a "holocaust to the Bible".
5. **Recorde.** Edward VI's court honours him, then the frame darkens to a prison cell. His colleagues laugh at his *Castle of Knowledge*: "vain phantasies." He is left to starve.
6. **Gilbert.** Before Queen Elizabeth with his lodestone globe. A goblet is set at his elbow. He drinks.
7. **The doubted.** Paracelsus, Agrippa and Dee in their studies, surrounded by whispering, sneering silhouettes.
8. **The sacrilegious hand.** Science's hand comes down on *De Magnete*, and on a veiled maiden of blue light, "The Heavenly White Virgin". The light dims under her hand.
9. **Bacon.** At the end of the gallery, in his Lord Chancellor's robes, Bacon looks back down the line of frames and pronounces them "Alchemicians of the Fantastic philosophy".
10. **Back to the study.** The empty frames remain. Sinnett, very quiet.

## Script

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sunlight again. Sinnett reads on, slower now. His eyes go to
the portraits and engravings on his own wall: a few framed
faces in a Victorian study.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     As hoary antiquity had more than one Socrates            [L62]

The camera drifts toward one dark frame on his wall, and
through it.
```

```
INT. THE GALLERY OF MARTYRS — TIMELESS

A long, high GALLERY, like a museum after closing. Dim,
vaulted, silent. On both walls, FRAMED PORTRAITS in heavy gilt,
oval and square, receding into darkness.

Between them, every few yards, an EMPTY FRAME. Gilded,
waiting, with nothing inside but dark wall.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …so the dim Future will give birth to more than one
     martyr.                                               [L62]

The camera passes one of the empty frames, and holds on it a
moment.
```

```
THE FIRST FRAME — ATHENS — EVENING

The frame's glass dissolves, and we are inside.

A bare stone cell. SOCRATES, old, broad-faced, bearded, in a
plain cloak, sits calm among weeping friends. A JAILER hands
him a CUP. He takes it as if it were wine, and drinks.

The friends turn away. Socrates does not.

The scene freezes, flattens, and becomes a portrait again.
```

```
INT. THE GALLERY — CONTINUOUS

Footsteps. Down the gallery walks SCIENCE: a tall woman in
pale classical robes, crowned with laurel, carrying an OIL
LAMP. Beautiful, cold, sure of herself. Her face is calm,
statue-smooth.

She stops before a frame: ARISTARCHUS OF SAMOS, robed,
pointing to a model of the heavens with the SUN at its centre
and the Earth set around it.

The frame opens. A hillside on Samos, sea and sky. Aristarchus
speaks to us, his voice ancient and clear.

                    ARISTARCHUS
          (the letter's quotation, L63)
     The Earth moveth circularly about her own centre.

The next frame: NICOLAUS COPERNICUS, centuries later, in his
tower, holding the same model, renewing the same idea.

Science looks at the two frames, the old and the new, and
TURNS HER FACE AWAY.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Enfranchised science contemptuously turned away her
     face from the Copernican opinion renewing the theories
     of Aristarchus Samius — who "affirmeth that the Earth
     moveth circularly about her own centre"               [L63]

She walks on. Her lamp light slides off the two frames, and
they fall into shadow.
```

```
THE THIRD FRAME — ROME — 1633

A vaulted tribunal. Red robes on a high bench. Before them,
GALILEO GALILEI, old, white-bearded, in black, kneeling, a
document in his hands.

Behind the bench, on the wall, the SHADOW of an ALTAR FIRE
rises and flickers, its flames reaching up behind the kneeling
man like a sacrifice prepared.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …years before the Church sought to sacrifice Galileo as
     a holocaust to the Bible.                             [L63]

Galileo's lips move, the words his own, unheard. The fire's
shadow towers over him. Freeze. Portrait.
```

```
THE FOURTH FRAME — THE COURT OF EDWARD VI — DAY

An OVAL GILT FRAME, a damask-blue background: ROBERT RECORDE,
bald, brown-bearded, in a dark buttoned coat, looking off to
one side as if listening.

The frame opens. A Tudor court, banners, the boy-king EDWARD VI
on his throne in crimson and fur. Recorde bows and offers a
BOOK: THE CASTLE OF KNOWLEDGE. The king accepts it.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     The ablest mathematician at the court of Edward VI —
     Robert Recorde —                                      [L64]

HARD CUT —

A PRISON CELL. Stone, straw, a high barred window. Recorde
alone, thin now, his coat hanging off him, an empty bowl at
his feet.

Outside the bars, his COLLEAGUES in academic gowns, the book
passed among them, laughing.

                    COLLEAGUE
          (the letter's quotation, L64)
     Vain phantasies!

They walk away, laughing. The book is tossed back through the
bars, and lands in the straw.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     …was left to starve in jail by his colleagues, who
     laughed at his Castle of Knowledge, declaring his
     discoveries "vain phantasies."                        [L64]

Recorde picks up the book. Holds it to his chest. Freeze.
Portrait.
```

```
THE FIFTH FRAME — THE COURT OF ELIZABETH I — DAY

A bright Elizabethan presence chamber. QUEEN ELIZABETH I, white
face, red hair, pearls, a great ruff, enthroned.

Before her, WILLIAM GILBERT, her physician, in a dark gown,
demonstrates a TERRELLA: a small round lodestone globe. A
compass needle beside it swings and settles. He tilts the
globe, showing how the Earth's axis holds steady as it turns.
The Queen leans forward, interested.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     Wm. Gilbert of Colchester — Queen Elisabeth's physician
     died poisoned, only because — this real founder of
     Experimental Science in England — has had the audacity
     of anticipating Galileo; of pointing out Copernican's
     fallacy as to the "third movement," which was gravely
     alleged to account for the parallelism of the Earth's
     axis of rotation!                                     [L65]

At his elbow, an unseen hand sets down a GOBLET of wine.
Gilbert, absorbed in his globe, lifts it without looking and
drinks.

The compass needle on the table begins to spin, wildly.

Freeze. Portrait.
```

```
INT. THE GALLERY — THREE FRAMES TOGETHER

Three frames side by side, each lit from within:

PARACELSUS, in a laboratory of flasks and fire, a long sword at
his side.
CORNELIUS AGRIPPA, among star-charts and occult diagrams.
JOHN DEE, long white beard, a black skullcap, gazing into a
polished black mirror.

Around the frames, in the gallery's dark: SILHOUETTES of
onlookers whispering behind their hands, shaking their heads,
pointing and smirking.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     The enormous learning of the Paracelsi, of the Agrippas
     and the De{e}s was ever doubted.                      [L66]

The three men do not look at the onlookers. They keep working.
```

```
INT. THE GALLERY — A PEDESTAL

SCIENCE again, in the centre of the gallery, beside a stone
PEDESTAL. On it: a great old BOOK, DE MAGNETE, open, its pages
of diagrams glowing softly.

Beside the pedestal stands a figure of pale BLUE LIGHT: a
young woman, veiled, made of the same Akás glow we saw in the
cave in scene 03. She is luminous, serene, almost a dream.

Science sets down her lamp. She lays her HAND flat on the open
book. The glow of its pages goes out.

She reaches out and lays the same hand on the shoulder of the
maiden of light. The blue light DIMS under her touch, fading
from the shoulder outward, until the figure is only a faint
outline in the dark.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     It was science which laid her sacrilegious hand upon
     the great work "De Magnete" — "The Heavenly White
     Virgin" (Akás) and others.                            [L67]

Science picks up her lamp and walks on.
```

```
INT. THE GALLERY — THE FAR END

At the end of the gallery, where it meets the light of a high
window: FRANCIS BACON, LORD VERULAM, in the black-and-gold
robes of the Lord Chancellor, a tall hat, a lace ruff.
Majestic, at ease, the great man of his age.

Science reaches him and stands at his side, a pair, like a
statue and its patron.

Bacon turns and looks back down the whole length of the
gallery: Socrates, Aristarchus, Copernicus, Galileo, Recorde,
Gilbert, Paracelsus, Agrippa, Dee. Every frame.

                    K.H. (V.O.)
     And it was the illustrious "Chancellor of England and
     of Nature" — Lord Verulam-Bacon — who having won the
     name of the Father of Inductive Philosophy, permitted
     himself to speak of such men as the above-named as     [L68]

                    BACON
          (the letter's quotation, L68; mild, final)
     Alchemicians of the Fantastic philosophy.

He turns away, into the window's light, with Science beside him.

Behind them, along the gallery, one by one the frames dim.

Only the EMPTY FRAMES stay faintly lit: waiting.
```

```
INT. SINNETT'S STUDY — DAY

Sinnett looks up from the letter at the framed engravings on
his own wall.

Between two of them, a pale rectangle where a picture once
hung: an empty space on the wallpaper.

He looks at it a long moment.

He turns the page.

CONTINUE INTO SCENE 07 (opens on L70).
```

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Character images** | Socrates `socrates.jpg`, Aristarchus `aristarchus-of-samos.jpg`, Copernicus `nicolaus-copernicus.jpg`, Galileo `galileo-galilei.jpg`, Recorde `robert-recorde.jpg`, Edward VI `edward-vi.jpg`, Gilbert `william-gilbert.jpg`, Elizabeth I `elizabeth-i.jpg`, Paracelsus `paracelsus.jpg`, Agrippa `cornelius-agrippa.jpg`, Dee `john-dee.jpg`, Bacon `francis-bacon.jpg`, Sinnett `alfred-percy-sinnett.jpg`. K.H. is voice only. Science, the maiden of light, the jailer, the colleagues, the tribunal and the onlookers have no images and are new designs |
| **The frames** | Recorde's anime image is already a portrait in an oval gilt frame on damask blue. Use it as the model for every frame in the gallery, so each martyr's anime image hangs as a framed portrait first and then opens into its scene. Descriptions of the other figures here are generic, since I haven't viewed those images yet. Check them before any shot list |
| **Science** | The allegory is built from the letter's own words ("turned away her face", "her sacrilegious hand"). Tall, laurel-crowned, pale robes, an oil lamp. Statue-smooth face, never cruel, just certain. She appears three times: turning away (L63), the hand (L67), beside Bacon (L68) |
| **The maiden of light** | "The Heavenly White Virgin (*Akás*)" uses the blue *Akás* colour from scenes 03 and 05, so the viewer recognises her as the same force. The dimming under Science's hand is the scene's key image |
| **Empty frames** | The scene's quiet idea: places waiting for "more than one martyr" to come. They stay lit at the end. Sinnett's own wall has an empty space too, connecting the gallery to his study. The letter doesn't say who the future martyrs are. The image only suggests the question |
| **Galileo's fire** | Only a shadow of an altar fire on the wall behind him, never real flames on him. The letter says the Church "sought to sacrifice" him |
| **Gilbert's goblet** | An unseen hand, so no one is accused by face. The spinning compass needle is the only sign of what happens |
| **Speaking parts** | Only three short lines, all quoted in the letter: Aristarchus (L63), a colleague (L64), Bacon (L68). Everything else is silent action |
| **Palette** | Gallery: dim gold frames on charcoal walls, the only warm light Science's lamp. Each frame has its own tone: Athens evening amber, Samos sea-blue, Rome red and fire-shadow, Tudor court crimson, prison grey, Elizabeth's court bright white and pearl, the three doubted scholars smoky green and gold. The maiden of light is the scene's only blue. Bacon's window: cold white daylight |
| **Text in frame** | Book titles (*The Castle of Knowledge*, *De Magnete*) and documents shown by shape only, not readable |
| **Pacing** | Slow and even, one frame at a time, like a museum walk. Give each frame the same rhythm: open, act, freeze. Speed up only at Recorde's hard cut from court to prison. Hold the longest on the maiden of light and on Bacon's last line |
| **Sound** | Gallery: echoing footsteps, Science's robe on stone, a distant clock. Athens: quiet weeping. Samos: sea and wind. Rome: murmured Latin, the crackle of the fire's shadow. Recorde: court trumpets, then a slammed cell door, laughter fading down a corridor. Elizabeth's court: lute music, the compass needle ticking, then spinning. The doubted scholars: whispers all around. The pedestal: a low hum from the maiden of light, cutting out as she dims. Bacon: silence under his line. Study: a clock, a page turning |
